# Changelog

One line per improvement run, newest first: date and time, what changed, why, which pages.

- 2026-11-13 19:30 — A job feed for job search engines (`/feeds/jobs.xml`): the 10,000 newest open jobs in the XML shape aggregators such as Jooble, Adzuna and Talent.com read (title, date, link, company, city, country code, remote, pay, job type, category, description), rebuilt on every run; the feeds page explains it. Why: aggregators send visitors to job boards that give them a feed, and the owner asked to be listed on all of them. Pages: `/feeds/`, `/feeds/jobs.xml`.
- 2026-11-13 16:30 — New look for the whole site: white background, Inter headings, pill buttons with a green main button, lists of jobs, tiles, category blocks and charts as rounded cards with a soft shadow, section links as pills. The home page hero is rebuilt: a centred headline, a large pill search, eight shortcut chips, four numbers, then a framed window with today’s jobs and four doors beside it (find my fit, resume builder, salaries, tools), and the employers’ logos as pills. The dark departures board and the separate “Latest tech jobs” section are gone (the window replaces both). Why: the owner asked many times for the look of the sites he sends (Rezi and others) and the paper-and-rules look never changed.
- 2026-11-13 15:00 — Crypto jobs hub rebuilt for the search “crypto jobs”: pay chart and rings, who is hiring (with logos), and a directory of narrower lists: by kind (remote, new, with salary, entry-level, internships), by profession (62 pages such as “Crypto compliance jobs”) and by place (37 pages such as “Crypto jobs in Singapore”). `/locations/` is now a country chooser: 51 countries grouped by part of the world, each with its number of jobs and of remote jobs, a filter box, then regions and cities. Why: the owner asked for our site to stand among the results for “crypto job” and for a choice of jobs by country like Jooble’s.
- 2026-11-13 14:10 — Pricing page (`/pricing/`, in the top menu): two separate tables of plans side by side, one for people looking for work (Free and Plus) and one for employers (Standard, Featured, Pinned), each with the price on top, features in groups and a tick, a cross or a few words in every cell; a switch picks the table; on a phone the feature name takes its own line so the paid column is never off the screen. The same tables stand on `/plus/` and `/post-a-job/`. Why: the owner could not find the comparison (it was a plain table far down one page) and asked for different tariffs for the two sides.
- 2026-11-13 13:20 — Resume examples: a page for each of 141 kinds of work (`/resume/examples/<role>/`) with an invented example written for that work, the skills today’s postings for it name (a chart, counted on every build), pay, share of remote jobs, the titles employers use, and a button that opens the example in the resume builder; a searchable directory `/resume/examples/`; links from role pages, the builder and the footer. Why: “<job> resume example” is the search that brings resume builders most of their visitors.
- 2026-11-13 10:34 — Guides (nine articles whose figures are recounted on every build), a help page with 24 answers and a filter, the open hiring index with badges for the top 50 employers, a facts page for investors and partners; tariffs with ticks for job seekers and employers, launch prices (about a third, the earlier price crossed out), a table of what the same things cost elsewhere; fifteen new tools; ten browser-side features (/ahead/). Why: the owner compared the site with hh.ru, Dice, Indeed, Rezi and RocketHunt and asked for a blog, a help centre, an employer rating, clear tariffs and lower prices. Pages: /guides/*, /help/, /open-hiring-index/, /investors/, /plus/, /post-a-job/, /tools/*, /ahead/.
- 2026-11-13 09:46 — Exact posting times and an hourly run: fresh jobs show “20 min”, “3 h” in lists and “35 minutes ago” on the job page (counted in the browser, so a page never states a stale age); the employers’ pages are read every hour at :17 UTC instead of once a day (a run takes 9–15 minutes); the copy says “every hour” where that is now true. Also the same day: charts on the rankings hub, the home page, category and role pages; company-against-company comparison; job feeds page; a poster for the owner’s Telegram and X channels (silent until he adds a channel and a secret); the Plus page as an offer with example screens; the resume builder in the main menu. Pages: all lists, /job/*, /status/, /insights/, /companies/compare/, /feeds/, /plus/.
- 2026-11-13 09:50 UTC — Tools page redesigned: the resume builder, job matching and application tracker are three wide rows with a working-style preview; the other 13 tools are a two-column ruled list (name underlined, one plain line, and a one-line worked example on the right) instead of 13 identical dark tiles, so the page is about half as tall and phone layout stacks cleanly. The salary example uses today's real median. Why: DESIGN_STUDY item 8 (tools page “looked like a wall of the same tiles”). Pages: /tools/ (tools_pages.py, style.css: `.twide`, `.tlist`).
- 2026-11-13 09:16 — Rankings counted from today’s postings: “companies hiring for <field> jobs” for each of the 23 fields, the 40 best paid remote jobs, companies with work-from-anywhere jobs, remote jobs that pay $100K+ by field; remote and entry-level versions of every category; remote contract / part-time / full-time lists; the remote hub links all of them. Why: the owner asked to take what FlexJobs has (hand-written “N companies hiring for remote X jobs” lists, type-of-work pages). Pages: /insights/*, /categories/*/remote/, /categories/*/entry-level/, /remote/.
- 2026-11-13 09:05 — Category directory: 23 categories and about 160 roles (not only tech: accounting, law, HR, sales, support, logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, education, retail, government, jobs by language), a page with text for every category (/categories/<slug>/), a plain line and a first FAQ answer on every role page, “Categories” in the menu, a category block on the home page; letter squares removed where a company has no logo; resume builder, job matching and tracker shown first among the tools; structured data and header icons made lighter. Why: the owner could not find where to pick a category and asked for a page per category that can rank. Pages: /categories/, /categories/*, /roles/*, home, /tools/.
- 2026-11-13 08:12 — “For you” rebuilt as a guided match: five plain questions (kind of work, experience, where, pay, skills) answered with buttons; every open job is checked against the answers and listed with ticks and question marks that say why it fits; hints show what similar jobs pay, which answer to loosen when few jobs fit and which skills the partial fits ask for; the country is prefilled from the time zone; no employer takes more than two rows in a row. Why: the owner asked for a JobAssist-style assistant that matches by pay, free and without sign-up. Pages: /for-you/. Also: `SOURCES_RESEARCH.md` (where other job sites get their jobs) and roadmap sections 29–35.
- 2026-11-13 15:30 — Contractor rate tool: the hourly comparison now reads "X% more than the $Y an hour that the same yearly income pays in a salaried job" and says what the difference pays for (days off, unbilled time, costs). Why: the old sentence was hard to follow (audit finding 22). Pages: /tools/contractor-rate/ (assets/tools.js).
- 2026-11-13 12:45 — The Board no longer shows an empty black wall: an unknown or mistyped skill, company or location in the address (and a feed with no open roles) now falls back to the latest roles, with a line in the footer saying which board was not found; if nothing can be loaded at all, the page says so and links to the job list. Parameters are also read case-insensitively (`?skill=Python` works). Why: the board is meant to run unattended on an office or classroom screen and is linked from widgets, so one typo left a blank screen with no explanation (audit finding 7). Pages: /board/ (build.py).
- 2026-11-13 04:40 — Companies directory: every employer row now shows a green "+N" mark (roles it posted in the last 7 days, by the employer's own posting date) next to its open-role count; three sort buttons (Most open roles, Most new this week, A–Z) that work together with the name filter; the lead became a direct answer computed from the data (who has the most open roles, how many employers posted this week and which led). Why: the page was a bare list of 544 names with one number, and "who is actually hiring this week" is the question a job seeker brings to it; the answer sentence is quotable by search and AI answers. Pages: /companies/ (build.py, site.js, style.css: `.up`, `.csort`). Sort buttons are hidden without JavaScript.
- 2026-11-13 01:30 — Salary by level pay ladder (intern, junior, senior, lead and above, no level in title: median, middle half, role count) with a one-sentence direct answer computed from the data (for example, how much more senior roles advertise than junior ones). Why: "what does a senior X make" is the most common salary question and we already hold the data; the sentence is quotable by other sites and AI answers. Pages: new section on /salaries/; on the /salaries/<skill, role or category>/ pages it replaces the plain two-column level table that landed in parallel (levels now in career order, with the middle half). New `.t4` table class keeps four-column tables inside a phone screen.
