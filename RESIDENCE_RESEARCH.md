# Residence permit in Europe and company registration

Research date: 13 November 2026.
For: a Russian citizen who lives in Vietnam and runs a small online business alone (English-language job board, income in crypto now, cards later).

**This is general information, not legal advice.** Rules for Russian citizens change often. Check the official page or ask a licensed immigration lawyer before you pay any fee or move.

How to read this file:

- Every statement has a source tag like [S8]. The source list is in section 5.
- "Official" means a government or provider page was opened today. "Secondary" means a law firm, news site or agency blog.
- Things that could not be opened or checked are listed in section 6. Do not treat them as facts.

---

## 0. Short answer

1. There is no EU-wide ban on residence permits for Russians. Each country decides. The Baltic states and Czechia are closed. Malta's nomad permit is closed. Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Hungary, Germany, France, the Netherlands and Cyprus have no nationality ban that I could find.
2. Almost every EU "digital nomad" permit needs proven income of about EUR 2,100 to 3,700 per month for the last 3 to 6 months. "Expected" income does not count.
3. The only European route with no income threshold is Serbia (register as an entrepreneur or a company). Serbia is not in the EU.
4. To apply at an EU consulate in Vietnam you need a Vietnamese residence card. The Spanish Embassy in Hanoi says this in writing [S8]. An e-visa or tourist status is not enough.
5. Stripe does not work for companies in Serbia, Armenia, Georgia, Montenegro, Turkey or Vietnam [S1]. For Stripe you need a company in a supported country (UAE, UK, US, Cyprus, Hong Kong and others).

### 1.3 EU-wide rules that affect Russians

- **Schengen visas:** on 7 November 2025 the European Commission ended multiple-entry Schengen visas for Russians. Each trip needs a new visa. This is about short-stay visas, not residence permits [S39].
- **Passports:** several countries no longer accept non-biometric (5-year) Russian passports. Finland from 1 June 2026, Latvia from 15 July 2025 (both official notices, seen at search level) [S38], [S33]. Use a 10-year biometric passport.
- **Payments:** the EU 19th sanctions package (October 2025) bans EU firms from giving card issuing, acquiring, e-money and crypto services to Russian nationals. Exempt: people with EU/EEA/Swiss citizenship or a valid residence permit there [S40]. A Serbian or UAE permit does not help. Details in section 4.

### 2.2 Details per country

**Spain, digital nomad visa (official sheet of the Embassy in Hanoi, file dated 24 August 2026) [S8], [S9]**

- For "qualified professionals": a degree, or at least 3 years of work experience.
- You work remotely for a company not located in Spain. Freelancers need a commercial contract.
- Your relationship with the company must be at least 3 months old.
- The foreign company must show "real and continuous activity for at least one year".
- Income: 200% of the Spanish minimum wage (SMI). The Embassy table says EUR 2,442 per month from 19 February 2026. Reports from applicants inside Spain say EUR 2,849 per month (EUR 34,188 per year) [S10]. Plan for the higher number.
- Criminal record certificate from every country where you lived in the last 2 years. Legalised or apostilled, translated into Spanish.
- **If you are not Vietnamese, you must show a Vietnamese temporary or permanent residence card.**
- Applications in Vietnam go through BLS in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Legal decision time is 10 days.
- The visa is for 1 year. Inside Spain you can ask for a 3-year permit, then renew for 2 years.
- Russians: a first-hand report says a Russian sole trader got the permit after applying inside Spain, approved in 19 days. Russian bank statements were accepted with a bank stamp [S10]. A law firm says the main problem is opening a Spanish bank account [S11].
- To apply inside Spain you first need a Schengen visa. Since November 2025 Russians get single-entry visas only [S39].

**Portugal, D8 and D7 (official visa page) [S12], [S13]**

- D8: average monthly income for the last 3 months of at least 4 minimum wages. The 2026 minimum wage is EUR 920, so EUR 3,680.
- D8 accepts a work contract, a "society contract" or service contracts. You must also show your tax residence.
- D7 is for pensions and income from property, intellectual or financial assets. Income from running your own active business does not fit.
- Processing is slow. Guides from June 2026 report 6 to 9 months because of the AIMA backlog.
- Citizenship now needs 10 years of residence (law in force since 19 May 2026). Permanent residence is still 5 years.

**Italy, digital nomad visa (official consulate sheet, file dated 16 December 2025) [S14]**

- Only for "highly skilled" workers. Proof: a degree recognised through CIMEA or a Declaration of Value, or 5 years of documented experience (3 years for ICT specialists).
- Income at least EUR 24,789 per year from the work you will do in Italy. Passive income is not counted.
- At least 6 months of previous work in the field (tax returns, client invoices).
- A registered lease or deed for a home in Italy, in your name, for the whole visa period. Hotel or invitation is not accepted.
- You apply at the consulate where you have legal residence. A tourist-type status is not accepted.
- The permit is for 1 year and renewable in Italy.

**Greece, digital nomad visa [S15]**

- EUR 3,500 per month.
- Since February 2026 (Law 5275/2026) you can no longer apply inside Greece. You must apply at the Greek consulate in your country of residence. Source is secondary.

**Hungary, White Card (official factsheet) [S16]**

- For people who work for a foreign employer, or who "own a share in a company with verified profits abroad" and manage it from Hungary.
- EUR 3,000 net per month for at least 6 months before entry. Proof: a 6-month bank statement or an income certificate.
- Apply at a Hungarian consulate in your country of nationality or habitual residence. Decision within 30 days.
- Valid 1 year, one extension for 1 more year.
- No family reunification. No national residence card. No other permit type during or after it. This is a 2-year stay, not a path to settle.

**Germany, freelancer permit (official Berlin page) [S17]**

- Covers freelance professions only: scientific, artistic, literary, teaching and similar.
- A job board is a commercial business, not a freelance profession. It would fall under the business-founder rules, which look at benefit for the German economy.
- Needs German health insurance, a revenue forecast, and letters of intent from clients. Over age 45 you must also show pension provision.
- The Berlin office accepts applications from people who already hold a German residence title.
- Poor fit for this case.

**France [S18]**

- "Entrepreneur / profession libérale" card: a viable activity in France and resources at least equal to the French minimum wage.
- "Talent, business founder": invest at least EUR 30,000 and hold a master's degree or 5 years of experience at that level. Multi-year permit of up to 4 years.
- EU long-term residence after 5 years, with a French language test.
- French language is needed in practice. Poor fit without French.

**Netherlands (official IND pages) [S19], [S20]**

- Self-employed: points test by RVO on experience, business plan and added value. The product or service must be new to the Netherlands. Freelancers need Dutch clients. Fee EUR 423. Decision in 90 days, can be extended by 6 months.
- Start-up: needs a contract with an approved facilitator. Valid 1 year, not extendable. Then you must switch to the self-employed permit.
- Poor fit for a solo job board.

**Cyprus [S21]**

- Digital nomad permit: EUR 3,500 net per month. Owners of a foreign company qualify if income is paid to them personally (salary or dividends).
- No work for Cypriot clients.
- You apply inside Cyprus after legal entry, in person in Nicosia.
- 1 year, renewable up to 3 years in total. No automatic permanent residence.
- Yearly cap. It was raised to 1,000 permits in October 2025 according to a law firm. I could not confirm whether the 2026 quota is still open.
- Company-based permit ("foreign interest company"): needs EUR 200,000 in share capital in one of the two routes. Not realistic here.

**Malta [S22]**

- Nomad Residence Permit: EUR 42,000 gross per year. Employees, company owners and freelancers qualify.
- **Russian and Belarusian applicants are not eligible** (official FAQ, 17 April 2026).

**Serbia [S23], [S24], [S25], [S26]**

- Russians enter visa-free for 30 days (article of 29 October 2026).
- You register as an entrepreneur (preduzetnik) or form a company (DOO). Then you apply for the "unified permit" (residence plus work) on the basis of self-employment. The official portal lists self-employment as a ground.
- Visa-free nationals can apply online, also from abroad. Physical presence is not required to file.
- Permit can be granted for up to 3 years and extended.
- Decision: 15 days per one law firm page, 30 days per another.
- Permanent residence after 3 years of temporary residence (law firm source, law changed in 2024).
- No minimum income or capital was found. Costs and taxes were not verified.
- Caveat: Serbian police have refused or cancelled permits of some Russians on "security" grounds, mostly anti-war activists. Cases from 2023 and 2024 are documented.
- Caveat: Serbia is under EU pressure to align its visa policy. The President denied plans for visas on 2 June 2026.

**Montenegro [S27], [S28]**

- Visa required for Russians since 1 November 2026.
- New Law on Foreigners rules (agency blog, 28 February 2026): a company owner must show at least EUR 5,000 per year in taxes and contributions to renew. For 51% owners and sole proprietors this applies from renewals in 2027.
- Digital nomad permit: income figures differ by source (about EUR 1,400 to 1,600). The programme is reported to run only until the end of 2026.

### 2.3 Is crypto income accepted as proof?

- No official page that I opened mentions crypto.
- Every official checklist asks for bank statements, contracts, invoices, payslips or tax returns [S8], [S12], [S14], [S16].
- Practical reading: crypto that sits in a wallet is not proof. Convert it to fiat through a KYC exchange, receive it on a personal bank account every month, and keep invoices. One forum thread from April 2026 says crypto salary "is possible but requires careful presentation". That is an opinion, not a rule.
- Note the EU sanctions rule in section 4: EU crypto firms may not serve Russian nationals without EU residence.

## 3. The three most realistic routes

| # | Route | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Serbia: entrepreneur or company, unified permit** | The only route with no income threshold. Visa-free entry. Online filing. Permanent residence in 3 years. Large Russian-speaking community. |
| 2 | **Spain: digital nomad permit** | Proven to work for Russians in 2026. 3-year permit, PR in 5 years. Needs EUR 2,442 to 2,849 per month and a company that is 1 year old. Use it once the business earns enough. |
| 3 | **Cyprus: digital nomad permit** | Russians are the main user group. English and Russian are widely used. Needs EUR 3,500 net per month. Limited to 3 years, no PR. |

Backups: Italy DNV if you have a recognised degree and want the lowest EU income bar (about EUR 2,066 per month). Hungary White Card if you only need a 1 to 2 year EU stay. Outside Europe: Armenia (fast and cheap) or UAE (company plus residence plus Stripe).

What to prepare, for any of them:

1. A 10-year biometric passport with long validity. Italy asks for 15 months, Spain for 1 year.
2. Legal residence in Vietnam (residence card) if you want to apply from Vietnam. Otherwise you apply in Russia, or inside the country where that is allowed (Spain, Serbia, Cyprus).
3. Criminal record certificates from Russia and from Vietnam, apostilled or legalised, with certified translation.
4. A registered business. For Spain it must be at least 1 year old, so the clock starts when you register.
5. A contract between you and the business, and invoices from customers.
6. 6 to 12 months of bank statements with regular fiat income on your personal account. Aim for EUR 3,000 or more per month to cover most routes.
7. A diploma with apostille, or documents that prove 3 to 5 years of work experience.
8. Health insurance valid in the country.
9. A translator or a Russian-speaking immigration lawyer. All forms are in the local language or English.

For Serbia only: items 1 and 3, a Serbian address (rental contract), and registration of the entrepreneur or company. Exact document list was not verified.

## 4. Where to register the company (for Stripe later)

### 4.1 What Stripe says

- Stripe lists these supported countries, among others: UAE, UK, US, Cyprus, Estonia, Hong Kong, Malta, Hungary, Spain, Portugal, Greece, Thailand. **Not listed:** Serbia, Armenia, Georgia, Montenegro, Turkey, Vietnam [S1].
- "Stripe currently does not support users located in Russia, Ukraine and Belarus." The page speaks about location. It says nothing about citizens who live elsewhere [S2].
- To open an account in a country you need: a legal entity registered there, a tax ID, "a physical location in that country where you can receive mail, not a P.O. box", a phone number, a website, and "a physical bank account in that country". ID can be "from any country" [S3].
- If you live in a different country than the account, only a passport is accepted as ID [S4].
- Stripe gives no public rule for Russian passport holders abroad. I found one unverified report of account freezes because of Russian passports. Treat approval as uncertain.

### 4.2 The EU sanctions rule (important)

- EU 19th package, in force since late 2025: EU payment firms may not provide acquiring, payment cards, payment initiation, e-money or crypto services to Russian nationals or residents of Russia [S40].
- Exempt: holders of EU/EEA/Swiss citizenship or a valid residence permit there. Long-stay D visas also count, per a Commission clarification of 13 March 2026 (secondary summaries) [S40].
- Per a law firm summary of that clarification: a company registered in the EU or a third country is not automatically covered just because its owner is Russian. But using a company to get around the ban is circumvention [S40].
- Meaning for you: an EU sole-trader account with Stripe, Wise, Revolut and similar is blocked until you hold an EU residence permit. A company account is legally possible, but each provider decides.
- Wise blocked cards of Russian customers who could not prove EEA or Swiss residence by 30 January 2026 [S40].

### 4.4 If Stripe is not available: merchant of record

- A merchant of record sells to the customer in its own name and pays you out. You do not need a Stripe account.
- Polar lists Vietnam, Serbia, Armenia, UAE, Cyprus and Thailand as payout countries. Georgia and Montenegro are not listed. Polar says it does not support "countries with US sanctions", and names Russia. It speaks about country, not citizenship [S42].
- Paddle, Lemon Squeezy and others were not checked today.

### 4.5 Reading of the evidence

Most workable for a Russian citizen who lives outside Russia and outside the EU:

1. **UAE free zone company.** Stripe works, Russians are accepted, and it also gives a residence visa. It costs the most and the bank account takes weeks.
2. **UK Ltd.** Cheap and fast, Stripe works. The open question is the UK bank account and UK address.
3. **US LLC.** Stripe works and Atlas accepts founders "from anywhere in the world". The open question is the bank: some US fintech banks refuse Russian citizens.

If you move to Serbia or Armenia, a local entity plus a merchant of record is the simple path, but it is not Stripe.
