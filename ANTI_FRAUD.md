# Keeping fraud out: what others do and what we do (2026-11-13)

Asked by the owner: "think the fight against fraud through; look at how Indeed and the other job companies do it; fraud must be
removed for sure". Sources: the platforms' own trust pages and transparency reports, FTC, FBI IC3, the UK government's job-scam
guidance, BBB, and write-ups of malware campaigns aimed at crypto job seekers. Marks: ✅ in place, 🔧 being built, 📋 planned.

## What the big sites do

| Site | How it checks an employer | How it screens a posting | What it publishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indeed | An identity check for employers | Automatic and human review; bans any cost to the job seeker | Second half of 2025: 529,000+ accounts removed automatically, 325,000+ by staff |
| LinkedIn | The poster confirms a work email of the company; extra checks for risky posters | A risk score per job; flagged jobs wait for a person | 98.7% of detected scam content stopped before a report; 90% of reported scam messages try to move people off the platform |
| Upwork | ID check | Models remove bad posts within seconds; every report is reviewed | Median review of a report: 22 minutes |
| Handshake | Government ID, legal name and registration number | Review, usually within a working day | |
| Wellfound | ID and a selfie check | Bans fees, outside forms and chat apps | |
| FlexJobs | Vetted employers | Every job read by a person | |
| CryptoJobsList | Asks a doubtful poster to confirm through the company's official channel | Automatic flags | |

Paying for a listing proves nothing: paid listings on crypto boards were used to move applicants to Telegram and then to a
"meeting app" that carried a password stealer.

## The schemes regulators describe

Pay to get the job (training, equipment, checks); pay to get paid ("task" jobs, then a deposit); a cheque that is too big;
forwarding parcels or money; an offer with no interview and pay that does not fit; early requests for ID, bank or card details;
a real company's name with changed contacts; a free mailbox or look-alike address; a move to Telegram or WhatsApp; a "test task",
"meeting app" or script that installs malware (the FBI and the US Department of Defense Cyber Crime Center describe this campaign
against developers and crypto workers).

## Our three layers

### 1. Listings read from employers' own systems (all listings today)

- ✅ Each job is read from the employer's hiring system and re-read every day; a job that disappears is marked closed that day.
- ✅ Every job page shows the source, the date of the last check, and now the address the Apply button opens.
- ✅ No messaging on the site and no resume database, so there is no way to approach a job seeker through us.
- 📋 The list of companies we read stays hand-picked; a new company's domain age and look-alike name are checked before it is added.

### 2. Jobs posted by employers themselves (server written, not switched on)

- ✅ One company name belongs to one account; the apply link is opened and checked; limits; a ban takes everything down.
- 🔧 Work email on the company's own domain only, confirmed by a link; free mailboxes refused.
- 🔧 The apply link must be on the same domain as the email, or on a known hiring system (then a person looks first).
- 🔧 Look-alike names of well-known companies go to review.
- 🔧 Every posting is screened before it appears. Refused outright: links to chat apps, form builders, link shorteners, file
  hosts and downloads; any fee asked of the candidate; cheques, gift cards, parcel or money forwarding; requests for a seed
  phrase, private key, passport, bank or card details. Scored and held for a person: a Telegram or WhatsApp contact, a free
  mailbox as contact, "like and rate" tasks, "earn $N a day", "no interview", requests to install or run something, pay far above
  the role, a company name in the text that differs from the account.
- 🔧 New accounts are held for review until two postings are approved and two weeks have passed; payment never skips review.
- 🔧 Reports without an account; one report about money or an install request hides a self-posted job until a person decides.
- 🔧 A monthly count of screened, refused, held and removed postings, published on the site.
- 📋 Needs a person: reading the review queue (the owner or someone he appoints), ideally within 24 hours.
- 📋 Not possible without a paid service: ID and company-registry checks like Handshake's.

### 3. What the job seeker sees

- ✅ `/safety/`: seven signs, four schemes spreading now, how to check a recruiter, crypto-specific checks, what we check and do not
  check, that we never contact anyone first, where to report in the US and the UK.
- ✅ "Report a problem with this listing" on every job.
- 📋 Warnings that appear only when a rule fires (for example "this employer joined less than 30 days ago"), once self-posting opens.

## Where this is stronger than the big sites

Listings come from the employer's own system by construction; the email, the apply link and the company site must agree; there is
nothing on the site through which a stranger can message a job seeker; rules against "install this" postings, which no general
board publishes; payment never buys trust.
