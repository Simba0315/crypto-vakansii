# Founders and company reports

Research date: 2026-11-13. All pages were opened on that date unless a different date is given.

**This is information for a later talk with a lawyer and an accountant. It is not legal, tax or financial advice, and it does not recommend any route.** Rules of payment services and sanctions change often; every rule below must be re-read at its link before anyone acts on it.

How to read the marks:

- Part A facts: **[own page]** = the company's own terms, privacy policy, pricing or about page; **[registry]** = an official company registry or a regulator filing; **[interview]** = the founder's own words in an interview; **[news]** = a news article.
- Part B numbers: **reported** (filing, earnings release, registry accounts, founder's own statement), **estimate by a third party** (named), **my own calculation** (arithmetic shown), **not found**.
- Official rules are given in close paraphrase with a link to the exact text. Only one sentence in this file is quoted word for word (the Estonian e-Residency rule); the rest is paraphrase so that nothing is misquoted. A lawyer should read the linked originals.

## 1. Summary (12 lines)

1. Eleven cases are sourced (ten from the companies' own legal pages, HeadHunter from a US regulator filing and news); five more are only partly verified and are listed separately.
2. The legal entities that appear are: US corporations (Careerist, Lemon.io, Huntflow, AmazingHiring), Cyprus companies (HeadHunter until 2024, Jooble's billing company, Mellow), Estonia OÜ (Relocate.me, Kwork, PeopleForce affiliate), Luxembourg S.A. (Job Today), Armenia (Huntflow's LLC, Hirify's individual entrepreneur), Singapore (Crypto Jobs List), Hong Kong and UK (Kwork).
3. No case in this set used a UAE free zone, Georgia, Kazakhstan or Serbia. They stay in the routes table only with what official pages say.
4. HeadHunter went the other way: Cyprus PLC, Nasdaq listing in 2019, delisted in 2023, moved back to Russia in November 2024.
5. Payments named in the companies' own documents: bank cards and invoices (Jooble, Lemon.io, PeopleForce, Job Today), consumer credit partners (Careerist), a separate UK reseller company that acts as the merchant (Kwork), an unnamed third-party processor with one-time payments (Hirify), Stripe plus crypto processors (Crypto Jobs List).
6. Estonia: the official page says first-time e-Residency applications from Russian citizens are not reviewed, and a residence permit in another country does not change that.
7. Stripe: its page says it does not support users located in Russia; I found no Stripe statement about Russian citizens who live elsewhere. Stripe also says people linked to an account must live in a country where Stripe works.
8. Paddle and Lemon Squeezy (merchant-of-record services): Paddle does not take suppliers operating from Russia or Belarus; Lemon Squeezy cannot take payments from customers in Russia and is being merged into Stripe's own merchant-of-record product, which lists 37 business countries.
9. Wise: an EU sanctions rule now covers Russian nationals as well as residents; cards were blocked unless the customer proved citizenship or a residence permit in the EEA or Switzerland.
10. Part B: only Upwork, Fiverr, SEEK, Adzuna, LinkedIn Premium, Recruit (Glassdoor's owner) and two founders' statements gave reported revenue. For most resume tools revenue is not public.
11. What job seekers pay for almost everywhere: tailoring a resume to one job, a keyword match score, cover letters, a job tracker. Typical price is 20 to 50 US dollars a month; two companies sell a pass with one payment and no renewal.
12. What employers pay for on small boards: free posting plus a paid boost (Wellfound from 200 dollars per ad, Handshake from 34 dollars a day, Relocate.me 230 to 320 euros a month per job slot).

## 5. Fifteen features worth taking, ranked

Ranked by how directly a company earns from the feature and how well it fits a small job board with a one-time paid plan and crypto payments.

1. **Resume tailored to one job in one click** (AI). Earns for Teal (29 dollars per 30 days), Huntr (40 dollars a month), Jooble's Fitly.
2. **Resume-to-job match score with missing keywords** (browser for the basic version). Earns for Jobscan (49.95 dollars a month).
3. **A pass with one payment and no renewal** (server, light). Used by Novoresume (23.99 euros for one month) and Hirify (6 dollars for one month). Fits crypto payments.
4. **Paid highlighting sold as a package: top of list plus a place in alert emails** (server, light). Earns for Wellfound (from 200 dollars per ad).
5. **Free job posting with promotion sold per day** (server, light). Earns for Handshake (from 34 dollars a day).
6. **AI cover letter for a chosen job** (AI). Part of Teal+, Huntr Pro, Kickresume.
7. **Job tracker and application manager** (browser). The free hook at Huntr and Teal; part of Hirify Plus.
8. **Jobs seen first and a filter by source** (server). The paid part of Hirify Plus; the board already reads employers' hiring systems.
9. **Short paid periods: week, month, quarter** (server, light). Teal sells 7, 30 and 90 days.
10. **Resume templates with PDF download** (browser). The core product of Zety, Resume.io, Kickresume, Enhancv, Novoresume.
11. **Job slots for employers by the month** (server, light). Earns for Relocate.me (230 to 320 euros a month per slot).
12. **Paid company pages** (browser, static pages). Earns for Glassdoor, Built In and Welcome to the Jungle, all by quote.
13. **Salary ranges per role from the board's own data** (browser, static pages). The traffic base of Levels.fyi, which then sells coaching from 1,250 dollars.
14. **Two or three levels of ad visibility** (server, light). SEEK's revenue per ad rose 18% in a year while ad numbers fell.
15. **Gift codes and a free plan for students** (server, light). Used by Kickresume.

Not taken: escrow and a percentage of payments (Upwork, Fiverr), pay per click (Jooble, Adzuna, Talent.com) and recruiter seats (Wellfound Reach, Blind). They need large traffic, a member database or handling other people's money.
