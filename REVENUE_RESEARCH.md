# What job sites really earn, and from whom

Research date: 2026-11-13. Every source below was opened on that date unless the row says otherwise.

## Summary (10 lines)

1. The large money is paid by employers. Recruit's HR Technology segment (Indeed + Glassdoor) reported US$9.67 billion revenue for the year to March 2026 at a 37.7% margin, and US$3.07 billion in the latest quarter (July-September 2026) at 47.6%.
2. Outside Indeed, employer-paid boards are flat or shrinking: ZipRecruiter fell from $904.6M (2022) to $449.0M (2025) with losses in 2024 and 2025; Dice revenue is down 13% year on year; HeadHunter cut its 2026 forecast to zero growth; CareerBuilder + Monster went bankrupt in June 2025 and its job boards sold for $28.4M.
3. The second pool is job seekers paying for resume tools. Filed accounts show Kickresume earned EUR 4.09M revenue and EUR 1.46M net profit in 2025; Rezi shows $238K monthly recurring revenue verified through Stripe. A lawsuit estimates BOLD (Zety, MyPerfectResume, LiveCareer, FlexJobs) at about $628M a year; that number is a plaintiff's allegation, not a report.
4. Seeker subscriptions for "jobs you will not find on LinkedIn" exist and are small: verified cases run from $0.4K to $20K a month. Remote Rocketship reached $6,000 a month 12 months after launch.
5. Small boards that tried to charge employers first mostly got nothing: Remote Rocketship made one $199 sale, Real Work From Anywhere ran about two years at $0, 4 Day Week had about six months without a sale.
6. JobAssist and RemoteJobsFinder publish no revenue. Both sell a cheap first period that renews automatically on a card at a much higher price, and both draw refund complaints (52% and 44% one-star reviews on Trustpilot; Trustpilot has withheld RemoteJobsFinder's score and removed fake reviews).
7. AI auto-apply is not proven to be profitable: AIApply claims 2 million users, but its filed UK accounts show accumulated losses growing by about GBP 661K in the year to October 2025. Indeed paused its own fully automatic apply in August 2026 after employer pushback (secondary source).
8. Hirify sells a one-time pass (399 RUB a month, not a subscription) for jobs parsed from company sites and says 3,500+ people bought it. This is the closest working example of the owner's payment constraint.
9. Google's job posting rules forbid postings that require payment from applicants and require job details to be visible without login, so the listing and the apply link have to stay free.
10. My reading: sell a one-time seeker pass for extras first, add ads/newsletter/affiliate income second, and employer featured posts third. No source supports a promise of income; year one for a board with no brand has ranged from about $0 to a few thousand dollars a month in the documented cases.

## How to read the confidence marks

- **reported**: company filing, earnings release, public register, founder's own post, or a revenue page verified through the payment provider, opened by me today.
- **reported (secondary)**: the company reported it, but I read it in a news article or a search-result summary; the primary document was not opened or could not be opened.
- **estimate (name)**: a third party's estimate. Not a fact.
- **claim**: an unaudited number the company prints on its own site (users, visitors, jobs).
- **my calculation**: arithmetic shown in the row or in the section "Revenue per 1,000 visitors".
- Currency conversion uses the Bank of Russia rates for 2026-11-13: 1 USD = 82.98 RUB, and 10,000 VND = 32.3851 RUB, so 1 USD = 25,623 VND (https://www.cbr.ru/scripts/XML_daily.asp).

# Conclusions (my reading, not facts)

Everything in this part is my interpretation of the tables above.

## 1. Where the money is

| Pool | Order of magnitude | Based on |
|---|---|---|
| Employers paying large platforms per result or per package | Billions of dollars a year for the leader; hundreds of millions for the next tier | Recruit HR Technology $9.67bn; SEEK A$1.2bn; ZipRecruiter $449M; HeadHunter RUB 41.2bn; DHI about $125M |
| Seekers paying for resume tools | Single millions a year for an independent builder; possibly hundreds of millions for BOLD | Kickresume EUR 4.09M (filed); Rezi about $2.86M a year (238,321 x 12); BOLD $628M is an allegation |
| Seekers paying for job access or copilots | Thousands to low tens of thousands of dollars a month for independents; about $10M a year for one venture-backed case | DirectRoles $20K, Remote Rocketship $6K at month 12, RareRoles $2.9K, Remote DXB $0.4K; Jobright $10M (founder statement, secondary) |
| Employers paying small boards per post | From $0 to a few thousand dollars a month unless the board owns a niche | CryptoJobsList $3-4K a month in 2018; Real Work From Anywhere one paid post in three years; Remote Rocketship one $199 sale |
| Advertising and newsletter | Up to about $5K a month in the cases found | Real Work From Anywhere over $5K a month from AdSense; 4 Day Week says newsletter ads are its largest stream |

Three observations:

- The employer pool is concentrating. Indeed grows by charging more per job while its rivals shrink, and US job volume is not growing. A new board does not get a share of that money by existing.
- Resume builders are the most profitable seeker business with public numbers (Kickresume keeps about 36% of revenue as net profit). They live on search advertising and automatic renewals.
- Several boards that used to charge only employers now also charge seekers: Remotive, Working Nomads, JustRemote, 4 Day Week. I read this as employer money getting harder for mid-size boards.

My range: most small boards made between $0 and about $10K in their first year. The cases that made $30K to $60K or more in year one all charged seekers and all had a traffic source that worked early (Reddit, TikTok, search). These are the cases founders chose to publish, so the typical result is likely lower than this table suggests.

My reading: a small board earns roughly $10 to $100 per 1,000 visitors; a platform with employer accounts and a sales force earns ten to thirty times more per visit. For this site, traffic is the first constraint, not the price list.

## 4. Which models fit one-time crypto payments

Fits:

- **A fixed-term pass sold once.** Hirify sells exactly this ("a one-time payment, not a subscription") and reports 3,500+ buyers. Remote DXB sells week, month, year and lifetime passes. Working Nomads and FlexJobs sell prepaid quarters and years.
- **Employer pay-per-post and prepaid bundles.** One-time by nature. CryptoJobsList states "We never make recurring charges"; Cryptocurrency Jobs accepts crypto; Remote OK's founder said bundles were about 30% of revenue in 2022.
- **One-time services or credit packs.** ApplyAll sold $883K of $249-$299 one-time packages; AIApply sells apply credits in packs.
- **Sponsorship, newsletter ads, affiliate commissions.** The advertiser or partner pays the site, so the seeker's payment method does not matter. 4 Day Week and NoDesk both earn from auto-apply partners.

Does not fit:

- **Cheap trial that renews automatically at a higher price** (JobAssist, RemoteJobsFinder, FlexJobs and the BOLD brands). It needs a stored card. Much of the complaint volume is about renewals people did not expect, which suggests a large part of that revenue comes from them. Crypto payments cannot pull money, so this revenue does not exist for this site.
- **Weekly plans.** They work on cards because renewal is silent. In Remote Rocketship's test, 55% of buyers chose the weekly plan and value per customer was $35-45; with manual renewal I expect fewer repeat payments.
- **Pay-per-click or pay-per-application billing of employers.** It needs metering, credit and disputes handling at Indeed's scale. I found no small board doing it.

Consequence, in my reading: without automatic renewal, expect revenue per paying seeker closer to one purchase (Hirify's $5-24) than to the $35-45 lifetime value Remote Rocketship reported on cards. The upside is no chargebacks.

## 5. Risks seen in the sources

- **Refund and renewal complaints.** JobAssist: 52% one-star, reviewers describe a $5.99 trial followed by $59.99. RemoteJobsFinder: 44% one-star, refund only after 100 documented applications, Trustpilot score withheld and fake reviews removed. LazyApply: 58% one-star, unanswered refund requests, reviewers planning bank disputes. BOLD faces an antitrust suit that also describes trial-to-renewal pricing.
- **Auto-apply economics and backlash.** AIApply's accounts show growing losses. Sonara closed in February 2024. Indeed paused its fully automatic "Apply for Me" in August 2026 after employers objected to paying for bot applications (secondary: https://resumehog.com/blog/posts/tool-review-why-indeed-paused-its-ai-auto-apply-feature.html , search summary). Workable says about 10% of applications on its platform come from mass-apply tools (https://resources.workable.com/inside-hr/stories-and-insights/ai-mass-apply-tools-hiring-signal-vs-noise/ , search summary). Promoting auto-apply partners ties the site's name to this.
- **Google for Jobs rules.** Google's documentation says: "We don't allow job postings that require payment from applicants", "Users must be able to see the job posting details without the need to login", "We don't allow job postings that don't have a way to apply", and expired postings must be removed. Violations can bring a manual action and removal from job search (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/job-posting). A paywall on the listing or the apply link is therefore a risk; paid extras around a free listing are not addressed by these rules.
- **A paywall costs traffic.** Remote Rocketship's first paywall test cut traffic by 50%.
- **Dependence on Google.** 4 Day Week lost 30-40% of traffic after Google updates. Semrush shows Remote Rocketship down 33% and Indeed down 23% from September to October 2026 (outside estimates, one month).
- **Employer demand is cyclical.** ZipRecruiter lost half its revenue in three years (-50.4%, my calculation: 449.0 / 904.6 - 1). CareerBuilder + Monster's job boards sold for $28.4M.
- **Unverifiable marketing numbers are common.** JobAssist prints "4.8 from 870 reviews" against 2.9 from 68 on Trustpilot. Copying that style would put this site in the same complaint category.

## 6. Recommended order for this site

No step below promises income. Each rests on the evidence named.

1. **First: a one-time seeker pass for extras, with every listing and apply link free.** Extras could be instant alerts, early access to new jobs, saved filters, salary data. Evidence: Hirify (one-time pass, 3,500+ buyers, same idea of jobs parsed from company sites); Remote Rocketship ($6,000 a month at month 12, after employers would not pay); DirectRoles and RareRoles (verified seeker revenue for "jobs from company sites"); 4 Day Week Pro (keeps browsing and applying free). Price evidence: $5 a week to $29 a month abroad, 399 RUB a month at Hirify; offering week, month and year together doubled Remote Rocketship's revenue against a single plan. Keeping listings free follows from Google's rules and from the 50% traffic loss in the paywall test.
2. **Second: income that does not depend on the seeker's payment method** - display ads, a jobs newsletter with sponsors, affiliate links to resume tools. Evidence: Real Work From Anywhere (same data method as this site, over $5K a month from AdSense after two years of nothing from employers); 4 Day Week (newsletter ads are the largest stream; it also earns from affiliates); Kickresume and Rezi show resume tools have the margin to pay partners. Caution: auto-apply partners carry the complaint risk described above.
3. **Third: employer featured posts and bundles for a one-time crypto payment, once traffic can be shown.** Evidence for the price: $199-$399 per 30 days across Remote OK, WWR, Working Nomads, NoDesk, Dynamite Jobs, Cryptocurrency Jobs, CryptoJobsList. Evidence for doing it last: Remote Rocketship sold one post at 60,000 visitors a month; Real Work From Anywhere got its first paid listing in its third year; 4 Day Week's founder says job ads are not one of his larger streams. Crypto-native employers are the likeliest early buyers, since crypto boards already take crypto.

Not recommended on this evidence: a cheap trial that converts to a recurring charge (impossible with crypto and the main source of complaints), and building an own auto-apply product (losses at AIApply, closure of Sonara, Indeed's retreat).
