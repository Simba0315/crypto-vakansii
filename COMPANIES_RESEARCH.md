# Companies research: money, legal entities, payments

Research date: 13 November 2026. Every page named below was opened on that date, unless the line says "search result only" or "not opened".

This file adds to `FOUNDERS_AND_REPORTS.md` and does not repeat it. It is information for a talk with a lawyer and an accountant. It is not legal, tax or financial advice.

How to read the marks:

- **own page** = the company's own site (terms, privacy policy, offer, pricing, checkout).
- **registry** = an official register or regulator filing (US SEC, UK Companies House, Google Ads Transparency Center).
- **founder** = the founder's own words in an interview or a company press release.
- **news** = a news article.
- **third party** = a data site or an estimate. Not a company figure.
- **search result only** = I saw it in a search result and did not open the page. Treat as unverified.
- **not published** = I found no figure from the company, a registry or the founder.

No figure in this file is my estimate. Where I did arithmetic, the line says so and shows it.

## Summary (10 lines)

1. Only one company in the founders file publishes full accounts: HeadHunter. Revenue was 41.2 billion rubles in 2025, net profit about 18 billion rubles.
2. For the others, the only money facts are funding rounds, founders' own statements, and registry data. Most revenue is not published.
3. Hirify is run by an individual entrepreneur in Yerevan, Armenia. "Armenia" is correct. Its site code shows three ways to pay: a Russian or Belarusian card in rubles, an international card in US dollars, and crypto by writing to the support bot.
4. Hirify says on its own page that more than 3,500 people bought Plus and that it has no investors.
5. In the table of 29 sites, the named card processor is almost always Stripe. Crypto is accepted only by Crypto Jobs List (through NowPayments, for selected customers) and by Hirify (by hand, through support).
6. Two sites sell through a "merchant of record" (another company is the legal seller): FlexJobs (Auxiliant S.à.r.l, Luxembourg) and remotejobsfinder.co (PayPro Global, Inc.).
7. remotejobsfinder.co is run by EXPERT REMOTE INC., Delaware. It sells one thing: a subscription that renews by itself. The advert leads straight to a price page with a countdown and "50% off".
8. Google's Ads Transparency Center shows about 1,000 adverts that link to remotejobsfinder.co and about 2,000 adverts for the advertiser in total.
9. Crypto.jobs is no longer a separate site. The address redirects to cryptojobslist.com.
10. HeadHunter was a Cyprus company listed on Nasdaq from May 2019. Trading stopped in February 2022, the listing ended in 2023, and the company moved to Russia in 2024. A listing is a choice, not a duty.

## B. Hirify (hirify.me)

**Who runs it.** The offer page names an individual entrepreneur, not a company: Igor Vladimirovich Alentyev. Registration number 264.1223758. Registered on 15 March 2022. Address: Komitas Avenue 51, apartment 76, Arabkir, Yerevan 0014, Armenia. Phone with the Armenian code +374. Source: https://hirify.me/oferta

**Law.** The offer and the terms are under the law of the Republic of Armenia. Disputes go to Armenian courts. Source: https://hirify.me/terms-of-service

**Other pages.** The privacy policy names no operator. It refers to Armenian law and to the offer. It lists Yandex Metrika, Google Analytics and Amplitude. It says the service does not store card data. Source: https://hirify.me/privacy-policy . The footer shows only "© 2026 Hirify.me", no company, no address, no payment logos.

**So "Armenia" is correct.** It is an Armenian individual entrepreneur, not an Armenian company. I did not check the Armenian state register itself.

**Prices** (https://hirify.me/plus): 399 rubles for one month, 990 rubles for three months, 1,990 rubles for a year. The offer page also gives 6 US dollars for a month. One payment, no automatic renewal. Refunds only for a technical error or if access was not given.

**Payment methods.** The legal pages say only "a payment provider". The price buttons open a sign-in window, so I did not see the checkout. The site's own public script contains the payment chooser. It lists three methods:

| Method shown to the buyer | Text in the script | Currency | Internal name in the script |
|---------------------------|--------------------|----------|-----------------------------|
| Russian / Belarusian card | "MIR, Visa, Mastercard from Russian and Belarusian banks" | rubles | `pally` |
| International card | "Visa, Mastercard from banks worldwide" | US dollars (an older text line says Armenian drams) | `polar` |
| Cryptocurrency | "Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT". The buyer is told to write to the support bot on Telegram. | USDT amount equal to the dollar price | `crypto` |

The same script has two status messages: "Checking payment status via Russian payment system" (labelled `robokassa`) and "Processing international payment" (labelled `arm`).

What this shows and what it does not:

- Robokassa is named in the script as the Russian payment system.
- `pally` and `polar` are internal labels. They look like the names of two payment services, but no page confirms it. **Processor for cards: not confirmed.**
- Crypto is handled by hand through support. No crypto processor is named.

## D. remotejobsfinder.co

**Who runs it.** EXPERT REMOTE INC., 131 Continental Drive, Suite 301, Newark, Delaware 19713, USA. The same name and address are in the terms, the privacy policy and the refund policy. Contact: contact@remotejobsfinder.co. Legal pages are dated 10 March 2024. Source: https://remotejobsfinder.co/en/legal/terms-of-use

Google's register confirms it: advertiser "EXPERT REMOTE INC.", country of registration United States, identity verified. Source: https://adstransparency.google.com/advertiser/AR15304368033691074561

Founder: not named on the site. A product listing site names a person as founder of a developer marketplace called "Expert Remote" (search result only). I could not confirm that it is the same company.

**What it sells.** One product: a subscription to a job-search service. The site promises to find jobs, tailor the resume for each job, write a cover letter and send applications for the user ("Smart auto apply", "50+ targeted applications every day"). There is a public list of jobs, mostly in the USA. It claims "1,000,000+ remote jobs" and "300k+" in the page title.

**Prices.** The price page set the currency by my location and showed Vietnamese dong. Prices in US dollars were not shown to me, so I do not give them.

| Plan | Price shown ("50% OFF") | What it renews at |
|------|-------------------------|-------------------|
| 1 week | 38,506.50 dong a day (crossed out: 77,013) | 539,091 dong every week |
| 1 month ("most popular") | 22,468.87 dong a day (crossed out: 44,937.73) | 1,348,132 dong every month |
| 3 months | 13,482.51 dong a day (crossed out: 26,965.02) | 2,426,852 dong every 3 months |

At the checkout the 1-month plan read: list price 1,348,132 dong, discount 674,066, tax 10% 67,407, **total today 741,473 dong**, then **1,482,945 dong every month** (tax included) from 13 December 2026 until cancelled.

Prices in dollars from other people (not verified by me): one Trustpilot reviewer wrote of a "$49.99 monthly fee"; a review blog wrote 39 dollars a month.

**How it earns.** Only from the subscription. I saw no adverts on the site, no paid job posts and no partner links. The "Apply" button on a job tries to open another page of the same site, not the employer's site.

**The road from the advert to payment.**

1. The home page has one main button, "Try Now".
2. "Try Now" and "Sign Up" lead straight to a price page. There is no free step and no questions first.
3. The price page shows a filled-in "profile" I never entered (goal, 5 to 9 years of experience, salary 80,000 dollars and more, fully remote) and says "We found 8,577 jobs that match your profile".
4. A timer counts down from 10 minutes: "50% discount reserved for".
5. It promises "Get a job in 4-6 weeks", "5-10 interviews", "1-2 job offers".
6. It shows logos of news sites ("Featured in") and of large employers ("Our members got jobs at").
7. The prices are given per day. The renewal amount is in small text under the button.
8. The button "Start Finding Jobs" opens the PayPro Global checkout. The first field is "Create account: E-mail". The pay button is labelled "TRY FOR FREE", while the same form says the card is charged at once.
9. I entered nothing and stopped there.

**Refund rule.** Money back only if the user asks within 30 days and proves with screenshots at least 100 applications over 14 days in a row (for monthly plans) or 50 applications over 4 days in a row (for weekly plans). Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal. Source: https://remotejobsfinder.co/en/legal/money-back-policy

**Reviews.** The site says "Rated 4.8 out of 5 based on 238 reviews". ## E. Cyprus and Nasdaq: the HeadHunter story

**Short answer to the owner's question.** "Cyprus" was the country where the group's top company was registered. "Nasdaq" is a US stock exchange where its shares were sold to the public. Neither is required to run a job site. A listing is a choice made by large companies that want to sell shares to many investors. ### E.2 Why Russian-founded companies used Cyprus

Only reasons that I could find in a source today:

1. **Low tax on profits sent from Russia.** HeadHunter's prospectus says the group applied a 5% tax rate under the Russia–Cyprus tax treaty to dividends, where the normal Russian rate is 15%. Source: SEC prospectus (management discussion).
2. **Cyprus taxes little at the holding level.** Gains from selling shares are "unconditionally exempt" from Cyprus company tax. Dividends from abroad are generally exempt. The company tax rate was 12.5% until 31 December 2025 and is 15% from 1 January 2026. Source: https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/corporate/income-determination and https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/cyprus/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income (reviewed 4 Aug 2026).
3. **Access to US investors.** A Cyprus company could list in the USA as a "foreign private issuer", with lighter reporting than a US company. Source: SEC prospectus.

Other reasons often named (an EU country, company law close to English law) I did not check against a source today.

### E.3 Why this stopped working

1. Russia asked to change the treaty. The change was signed on 8 September 2020. From 1 January 2021 the rate on dividends and interest is 15%, with 5% kept only for some listed companies and funds. Sources: https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2020-2198-cyprus-and-russia-sign-the-protocol-to-amend-double-tax-treaty ; SEC annual report.
2. In February 2022 Nasdaq stopped trading, and in 2023 it removed the shares.
3. The annual report for 2022 warned: "we may be forced to redomicile in Russia". In 2024 the group moved to a Russian special region made for such moves.
